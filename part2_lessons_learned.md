
# Governance Lessons Learned - Part 2

This simulation helped me understand that technical vulnerabilities are often connected to weaknesses in governance. The unpatched Struts vulnerability was not just a technical problem. It showed that a patch management policy may exist, but if it is not properly implemented and enforced, the organisation remains exposed. The same issue occurred with the database, where default credentials were still in use despite an existing vulnerability management policy. The attack also succeeded without triggering any detection, showing that having security policies and monitoring tools is not enough if they do not work together effectively. Out of the eight governance failures identified, two were directly related to patch management, while the other six reflected a wider problem with policies, metrics, and architectural decisions that lacked proper enforcement, monitoring, and accountability.

I can also see a clear connection between this simulation and the Equifax breach. Equifax had a patch management policy and received a vulnerability warning from US-CERT, yet the breach was not detected for 76 days. This shows that the problem was not simply a lack of information, but a failure to turn that information into timely action. The same lesson applies to this simulation. The vulnerability was identified by the scanner, and a policy existed to address such risks, but the necessary remediation did not happen before the attack succeeded. As a GRC analyst, I have learned that having a documented policy does not automatically mean that governance is effective. I need to look beyond the existence of policies and verify whether controls are actually implemented, whether risks are monitored using meaningful metrics, whether responsibilities are clearly assigned, and whether identified issues are resolved on time.
