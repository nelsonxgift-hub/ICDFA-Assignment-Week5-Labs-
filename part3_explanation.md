Governance Controls Explanation - Part 3
How the implemented controls address the Equifax governance failures (Part 3)
The controls implemented in this stage directly target the governance failures mapped against Equifax in Part 2. For patch management, I moved from an undocumented, unenforced process to a recorded control with a named owner and a compliance metric tracked against a 7 day target, addressing Equifax’s lesson that an available patch is meaningless without ownership and a deadline. For network segmentation and database security, I recorded controls restricting direct web to database access and removing default credentials, which support defence in depth and address Equifax’s lateral movement and weak authentication failures. For monitoring, I implemented alerting logic and a coverage metric to address the 76 day detection failure, and for oversight I documented executive and board responsibilities, reporting lines, and accountability mechanisms to address Equifax’s unclear accountability.
The re-run of the attack simulation after implementing these controls is an important finding in itself. The attack still succeeded, showing that recording a control in a governance tracker and assigning ownership is not the same as technically closing the vulnerability on the live system. This is the exact pattern that happened at Equifax: governance structures existed but did not translate into verified technical remediation before the breach. The lesson I am taking forward is that governance controls must be followed through to technical verification, not just documented, or the organisation is left with the appearance of control rather than its substance. My next step would be to investigate why the simulation still succeeded, identify which controls were not technically enforced, reapply the necessary fixes, and retain evidence until the risk is resolved or formally accepted.
